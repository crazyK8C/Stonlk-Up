# Pain points of inexperienced traders

## The evidence

| Finding | Source |
| --- | --- |
| 93% of over 1 crore individual equity F&O traders in India lost money in FY22–FY24. Average loss was about ₹2 lakh per trader, and aggregate losses exceeded ₹1.8 lakh crore. | [SEBI press release, Sept 2024](https://www.sebi.gov.in/media-and-notifications/press-releases/sep-2024/updated-sebi-study-reveals-93-of-individual-traders-incurred-losses-in-equity-fando-between-fy22-and-fy24-aggregate-losses-exceed-1-8-lakh-crores-over-three-years_86906.html) |
| Over 75% of F&O traders in FY24 declared annual income under ₹5 lakh. Only 8.3% of traders who kept going after two losing years made a profit in year three. | SEBI (same study, via [Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/invest/93-of-individual-traders-suffered-losses-in-fo-in-last-3-years-sebi-study)) |
| 97% of Brazilian day traders who persisted for more than 300 days lost money. Only 1.1% earned more than the minimum wage. The authors found **no evidence of learning** by day trading. | [Chague, De-Losso & Giovannetti, "Day Trading for a Living?"](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:fgv:eesptd:525) |
| US households that traded most earned 11.4% a year against 17.9% for the market (1991–96). Overconfidence explains the overtrading. | [Barber & Odean, J. Finance 2000](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/Papers%20current%20versions/Individual_Investor_Performance_Final.pdf) |
| Investors sell winners faster than losers (the disposition effect). 84% of Taiwanese investors do it. | [Odean, J. Finance 1998](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/disposition/disposition.html) |
| Stocks that Robinhood users herded into averaged −4.7% abnormal return over 20 days. | [Barber, Huang, Odean & Schwarz, J. Finance 2022](https://profiles.wustl.edu/en/publications/attention-induced-trading-and-returns-evidence-from-robinhood-use/) (figure from secondary summary) |
| Investors lost about 1.2% a year to poor timing of buys and sells, roughly 12% of fund returns over 10 years. The gap was about 14% in crypto ETFs. | [Morningstar, Mind the Gap](https://www.morningstar.com/business/insights/research/mind-the-gap-2025?con=9541) |
| 61% of US investors under 35 act on finfluencer recommendations. 51% of investors couldn't spot fraud warning signs. The average financial literacy score was 5.3 out of 11. | [FINRA Foundation NFCS](https://www.finra.org/media-center/newsreleases/2025/new-finra-foundation-research-examines-shifting-investor-behaviors) (figures via secondary coverage) |

## The eight pain points

| # | Pain point | Evidence | What the product does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| P1 | No safe place to practice | SEBI F&O losses | Free, unlimited simulations |
| P2 | Noisy feedback: outcomes don't reveal decision quality | Brazil study: no learning | Separate process, calibration and outcome scores; show the distribution of outcomes |
| P3 | Overconfidence and overtrading | Barber & Odean | Confidence sliders, Brier score, calibration curve |
| P4 | Can't size risk | SEBI income data | Position-sizing and risk-of-ruin drills |
| P5 | FOMO and herding | Robinhood study | Hype-feed pressure drills |
| P6 | Selling winners, holding losers | Odean | Required exit plans; flag deviations in the debrief |
| P7 | Bad information sources | FINRA | "Spot the red flag" modules |
| P8 | Panic in volatile markets | Morningstar | Blind crash replays; practice sticking to a plan through drawdowns |

## What builds confidence

Confidence that comes from winning is fragile, because winning is partly luck. The aim is **calibrated confidence**: knowing what you know, sizing bets to match, and following a plan. Evidence that this can be trained is in [04-learning-science.md](04-learning-science.md).

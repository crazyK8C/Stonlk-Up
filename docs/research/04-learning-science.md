# Learning science behind the guided approach

| Technique | Evidence | How Stonk Up applies it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Simulation-based learning** | Meta-analysis of 145 studies (about 10,500 participants): g = 0.85. Scaffolding and technology both helped. Results held across fields including management. [Chernikova et al., RER 2020](https://portal.fis.tum.de/en/publications/simulation-based-learning-in-higher-education-a-meta-analysis/) | The Strategy Lab itself. Scaffold heavily early (hints, worked examples), then fade. |
| **Debriefs** | 46 samples (N = 2,136): debriefs improve effectiveness by about 25% (d = 0.67). [Human Factors 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23516804/) | A structured debrief after every run: what you planned, what you did, what was knowable, what to change. |
| **Feedback, done carefully** | 607 effect sizes: feedback helps on average (d = 0.41) but **reduced performance in over a third of cases**. [Kluger & DeNisi 1996](https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/feedback-interventions-toward-the-understanding-of-a-double-edged/) | Aim feedback at the task and process ("your stop was 3× your risk limit"), never at the person ("you're bad at this"). Keep cat jokes kind. |
| **Retrieval practice** | g ≈ 0.61–0.70; classroom (0.67) matches lab (0.62); transfer 0.53. [Adesope et al., RER 2017](https://www.learningscientists.org/blog/2017/2/9-1) | Predict-then-reveal (already our core loop) and short recall quizzes in Study tracks. |
| **Spacing** | 839 assessments across 317 experiments: spacing reliably helps recall, but only about a third of intellectual-skill studies showed a benefit. [Cepeda et al. 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16719566/) | Space concept recall (definitions, formulas). Don't over-claim spacing for strategy skills; use varied practice there. |
| **Calibration training** | A training module under an hour improved forecast accuracy (Brier) by 6–11%; Tetlock reports about 10% gains lasting up to 4 years. [APS Observer](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/publications/observer/obsonline/predicting-uncertain-events-on-a-global-scale.html), [Open Philanthropy](https://www.openphilanthropy.org/wp-content/uploads/Philip_Tetlock_03-08-16_public.pdf) | A 30–45 minute "Think in odds" module in Phase A, plus a confidence slider on every prediction. |
| **Gamification** | g = 0.49 for cognitive, 0.36 for motivational and 0.25 for behavioural outcomes. **Game fiction and social interaction** drove behavioural effects. [Sailer & Homner 2020](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-019-09498-w) | Lean into story (the cat universe, scenario narratives) and social play (cohort challenges), not just points. |
| **Financial education works** | 76 RCTs with 160,000+ people: positive effects on knowledge and later behaviour, robust to publication bias. [Kaiser et al., JFE 2022](https://www.diw.de/de/diw_01.c.785790.de/publikationen/diskussionspapiere/2020_1864/financial_education_affects_financial_knowledge_and_downstream_behaviors.html) | Justifies the Study section's existence. Measure behaviour, not just quiz scores. |
| **Market simulation games** | RCT in about 600 classrooms: players scored significantly higher in math and financial literacy. [SIFMA / Learning Point](https://www.sifma.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/05/education_sifma-foundation_learning-point-study-summary.pdf) | Evidence that simulation games specifically can teach finance. |

## Design rules that follow

1. **Scaffold, then fade:** worked example → guided run with hints → independent run → pressure run.
2. **Every run ends in a debrief.** No exceptions.
3. **Feedback names the behaviour and the fix,** never the person.
4. **Ask for confidence on every prediction** and show calibration over time.
5. **Mix concept types** in practice sessions so players learn to choose the right tool, not just repeat one.
6. **Narrative and social layers** are part of the learning design, not decoration.

## What we don't know yet

There's no strong evidence that simulated-trading discipline transfers to real-money behaviour. We shouldn't claim it does. Measure what we can in the app (plan adherence over time, calibration error, rule breaks under pressure) and consider a small study with a university partner later.

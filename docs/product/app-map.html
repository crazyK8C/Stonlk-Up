Your question first

How do today's stock games and paper trading simulate the market, and are we doing the same?

Short answer: they mostly replay the present, with lots of fake money, and score you on returns. Stonk Up has no market simulation yet, so we aren't repeating those patterns. The one partial overlap is that XP today rewards only correct answers.

The line we draw

Trading is strategic study, not gambling.

Every feature should push the player toward the right-hand column.

Gambling habits

  • BeforeA hunch, a tip, or a hot streak
  • Bet sizeWhatever feels exciting
  • ExitNone. Hope, or panic.
  • After a lossChase it
  • Judged byDid I win?
  • LearningRemembers the wins

Strategic study

  • BeforeA written thesis and what would prove it wrong
  • Bet sizeA rule, such as 1–2% of the account at risk
  • ExitPlanned in advance and followed
  • After a lossDebrief: bad beat or bad decision?
  • Judged byWas the process sound, and was my confidence right?
  • LearningA journal, a calibration curve and the gaps

Illustrative shapes, not real data.

Plan gap

What you planned versus what you did. Measures discipline.

Behaviour gap

Your decisions versus simply holding through it. The same idea as Morningstar's investor-return gap.

Benchmark gap

Your mix or strategy versus a plain index over the same window.

Confidence gap

How sure you were versus how often you're right. This is your calibration.

Guardrail: the Event Lab reports what happened and asks reflection questions. It never says "you should buy, sell or rebalance". Historical prices need a data licence and must be at least 30 days old. Until a licence is in place, use synthetic, event-shaped scenarios labelled as models.
